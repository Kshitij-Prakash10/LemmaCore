# Study sheet: the problem and the evidence

## In one sentence
On conventional electric locos the auxiliary converter fails often and the standby sometimes does not take over, which leaves the loco pilot mid-section with no guide on what to switch off, how long is left, or whether to stop.

## Five facts to know (and where they come from)
1. RDSO "expressed serious concern about high rate of failures" of 180 kVA SIVs on conventional electric locos (meeting, 29 May 2025). Source: RDSO minutes, file 250606A. Trust: high.
2. FY 2024-25: 582 failures in 2,116 units, **about 27 per 100 units a year**. The table is primary; the total and "per 100" are **our arithmetic**. It covers conventional locos, not BLW's 3-phase locos. Source: Annexure-I of the same minutes.
3. Sheds reported load not shifting from SIV1 to SIV2 (Siemens 2x130 kVA), and not shifting when the front-panel MCB trips (Medha 2x130 kVA). Source: same minutes.
4. The fault log holds 20 days, but scheduled loco visits are 60 days apart, and the makers did not share root-cause analyses. Source: same minutes.
5. What exists: REMMLOT remote monitoring, CLW's analytics spec for 3-phase locos (April 2024, deployment unknown), and the loco's own thermal protection. **We found no** crew advisor. Source: IRIMEE slides, CLW documents. Trust: medium.

## Three hard questions and answers (no reading from a screen)
1. **Q: Is 27 per 100 really RDSO's figure?** A: No, it is our sum of their table. Their table lists failures and units per maker; we added them up. And it applies to conventional electric locos only.
2. **Q: Your judges are from BLW, which builds 3-phase locos. Is this relevant to them?** A: The RDSO data is for conventional locos. 3-phase locos also use auxiliary converters (a vendor lists 3 x 130 kVA), so we made the converter count a setting, 2 or 3. We have not validated on a 3-phase loco.
3. **Q: How do you know no crew advisor exists?** A: We don't. We searched and found none. REMMLOT reports health to the shed, and the protection trips the motor; neither tells the crew what to do.

## What I do not know, and how I will say so
- The cost per failure: "We don't have a cost figure and did not want to invent one."
- What crews do today: we found an old traction rule (try to resume within 20 minutes, else inform control), at medium-low trust. "We are asking the mentor to confirm current practice."
- The Vande Bharat incident (October 2019, three coaches without AC and lights for about an hour) comes from news only. "One news report, not an official record."
