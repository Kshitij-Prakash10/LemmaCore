What should the loco pilot do when the backup power fails?

What this app does, in plain words

On a loco, one unit (the auxiliary converter) powers the cooling blowers, the brake-air compressor, the battery charger and the lights. Many locos have a second one to cover for it. Sheds report that the cover does not always take over. This app shows what the loco pilot should do next, and proves the advice by testing every fault.

How to use it

Open the Advisor tab. Press the hard-case button, or pick up to two faults and set the conditions, then press Run advisor. The answer on the right says what to do, how many minutes are left, and what to switch off.

Open the Coverage map to see all 144 tests at once. Click any square to open that test in the Advisor.

How to read the answer

The large words are the decision: keep going, stop at the nearest safe place, or stop now. Minutes left is a cautious estimate, so the real time is usually longer. The red line in the chart is what happens without the advisor. The dark line is what happens with it.

What is real and what is assumed

Every loco number in this app is a team assumption until a railway expert confirms it. The Assumptions tab lists each one. The test results come from a simulation of our model, not from a real loco.

Where the problem comes from

The railway’s own technical body (RDSO) recorded serious concern about converter failures in May 2025. In its 2024 to 2025 table, about 27 failures per 100 units a year on conventional electric locos, our arithmetic. Sheds also reported that the standby did not take over.

Words used in this app

WordMeaning
Converter (SIV)The unit that makes power for the cooling blowers, compressor, charger and lights.
Standby, failoverThe second converter taking over when the first one stops.
BaselineWhat happens with no load shedding: the loco keeps going until its own protection trips.
AdvisorThe decision aid in this app. It picks what to switch off and says whether to continue or stop.
Minutes leftTime until the first limit: motor heat, brake air or battery. Shown as a cautious figure (5th percentile).
Fault injectionA test that applies every fault, one at a time and in pairs, and records what happens.
Proxy dataReal data from a similar machine, here a metro train compressor, used as a stand-in.
